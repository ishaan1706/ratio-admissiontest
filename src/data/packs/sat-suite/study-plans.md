# Choose a SAT study plan

Start from the test date and pick the longest plan you can realistically complete. A typical week is four 35–50 minute sessions plus one distinct block for timed practice or a full test when scheduled. Reserve 134 minutes plus the 10-minute break for a full SAT, and review it in a separate sitting. Plan one rest day after a full test if possible.

**Start with a baseline.** Use a fresh [Bluebook practice test](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests/bluebook) for a scored baseline when you have time. Otherwise pick a short timed Ratio set spanning all eight domains and use only its raw accuracy, pace and breakdown. For each miss, log a reason and a next action.

**Choose the next drill.** Each week rank skills by first-attempt accuracy when at least five fresh items have been seen; use the higher-frequency domains to break ties. With fewer than five attempts, sample more instead of declaring a skill mastered or weak. Keep two targeted blocks and one mixed block. If you miss a day, preserve due review and one timed set; drop some new questions instead of doubling the next day.

**Repeatable short block (40 min):** 5 min due vocab / rules recall → 10 min one skill lesson and cover-solve examples → 17 min a tightly filtered set → 8 min error log and next review. Get explanations after submitting, and solve a matched unseen item without peeking.

## 2 weeks — triage

| Week | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take a baseline immediately. Use two blocks on the clearest misses, one for a punctuation and transitions pass, and one for linear or nonlinear Math with exact and Desmos methods. | Sit one timed module in each section on separate days. Log every miss and flag guesses. |
| 2 | Redo due errors cold; spend new-item time testing the same skills. Sit a fresh Bluebook or Ratio mock early enough to review. Rehearse breaks, calculator and SPR. | Compare accuracy and pacing against week 1; stop heavy work the last day. |

## 4 weeks — convert weak points

| Week | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline and mistake categories; work Algebra and Boundaries first unless baseline points elsewhere. | Use a fresh set of about eight items at each priority skill before reranking. |
| 2 | Advanced Math plus Craft and Structure. Compare exact methods with Desmos; start daily vocab recall. | Time one Math module and review separate from the timer. |
| 3 | Information and Ideas, Expression of Ideas, Data Analysis and Geometry. Prioritize weak skills; alternate mixed and targeted sets. | Time an R&W module and check quote and table errors. |
| 4 | Take a fresh full practice test early; retest its weakest recurring errors with different bank items. Avoid starting major new topics late. | Rehearse pacing and test-day logistics. |

## 8 weeks — learn, then rehearse

| Week | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline, filter the bank, and learn the review loop. | Pick two priority skills per section from actual attempts. |
| 2 | Linear equations, functions, systems; R&W Boundaries and form. | Explain each algebra mistake in one rule. |
| 3 | Equivalent expressions and quadratics; Words in Context and purpose. | Try one worked example in each exact and graph method when applicable. |
| 4 | Ratios, percentages, tables and scatterplots; textual and quantitative evidence. | Time one module in each section across separate days. |
| 5 | Probability, sample claims, geometry and trigonometry; inference and paired texts. | Recheck the difference between sampling and assignment, and between an inference and certainty. |
| 6 | Transitions and synthesis; mixed hard and SPR practice. | Fresh timed full practice and error audit. |
| 7 | Work the top recurring errors; alternate timed modules with slower teach-back reviews. | Confirm fixes on unseen questions and compare first-try pace. |
| 8 | Final fresh Bluebook early in the week or use a reserved Ratio mock for timing. Review only recurring mistakes and logistics. | Reserve the last day for light recall and rest. |

## 12 weeks — build broad foundations

| Week | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline and schedule; start due vocab and error log. | Rank weak domains without overreading small samples. |
| 2 | Linear one/two-variable equations and linear models; R&W central ideas. | Explain slopes and intercepts with their units. |
| 3 | Systems and inequalities; Boundaries. | Check agreement between graph and exact solutions; contrast full and dependent clauses. |
| 4 | Equivalent expressions and nonlinear equations; Form, Structure, and Sense. | Time two short sets; note any slow correct answers. |
| 5 | Nonlinear functions and calculator fluency; Words in Context and text purpose. | Give two methods for a quadratic without relying on rounded screen values. |
| 6 | Ratios, rates, percentages and data summaries; quantitative evidence. | Revisit base quantities, row/column comparisons and units. |
| 7 | Probability, models, margin of error and study design; textual evidence. | Distinguish random assignment and random sampling. |
| 8 | Geometry and trig; Inferences and Cross-Text Connections. | Rehearse formula recall; time one module in each section on separate days. |
| 9 | Rhetorical Synthesis and Transitions; mixed Math SPR. | Take a full-length test and separate content gaps from pacing problems. |
| 10 | Lowest first-try skills; compare old errors with unseen variants. | Require a cold correct follow-up before treating a mistake as fixed. |
| 11 | Alternate timed modules and deliberate review; rehearse test-day routine. | Use different material from week 9 to measure progress. |
| 12 | Fresh official rehearsal at the start of the week; light final review and device/ID checks. | Prioritize sleep and an early end to heavy practice. |
