# SAT grammar and transitions: quick reference

## Identify the tested feature

- Different punctuation with the same words → **Boundaries**. Count independent clauses or identify a modifier/list.
- Different verb forms, pronouns, apostrophes, or modifiers → **Form, Structure, and Sense**. Find the subject, timeline, antecedent or actor.
- Different connecting phrases → **Transitions**. Name the logic before reading the choices.

| Rule | Correct example | What to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Two full clauses | “The case is heavy; it needs two handles.” Or “The case is heavy, so it needs two handles.” | A comma alone. |
| Linking adverb after a boundary | “The timer worked; however, its display was dim.” | “The timer worked, however, its display was dim.” |
| Dependent clause first | “Although the paint was dry, the room stayed closed.” | A period or semicolon after the although-clause. |
| Colon after a full clause | “We packed three tools: a punch, a file, and a hammer.” | “We packed: a punch, a file, and a hammer.” |
| Essential vs. nonessential description | “Members who signed up early used the large room.” “The large room, which was upstairs, held 40 people.” | Commas around a clause needed to identify the intended members; one unpaired comma around an internal aside. |
| Dashes and parentheses | “The spare hinge—an older design—still worked.” | Mixing dashes and commas as the opening and closing markers of a single interruption. |
| Shared subject | “The crew packed the case and checked the latch.” | Splitting the second verb into an incomplete sentence. |
| Agreement, not proximity | “A set of keys fits the cabinet.” “The keys in the cabinet fit the latch.” | Making “set” plural because “keys” appears nearby. |
| Timeline | “The ferry closed in June and reopened in July.” | Switching to present or future without a time cue. |
| Clear pronoun reference | “When the jar slipped, its lid loosened.” | “When the jar hit the bowl, it broke” if the broken object must be clear. |
| Possession | “One worker’s badge”; “two workers’ badges”; “the children’s shelf”; “its lid.” | Adding an apostrophe to a simple plural or writing “it’s lid.” |
| Modifier attachment | “Holding the key, Rosa opened the gate.” | “Holding the key, the gate opened.” The gate cannot hold the key. |
| Parallel forms | “They sorted, dated, and labeled the letters.” | “They sorted, dated, and were labeling the letters” if the actions share the same simple-past sequence. |
| Former/latter and comparisons | “The battery in this lamp lasts longer than the battery in that lamp.” | Comparing the battery with the other whole lamp when the intended comparison is battery life. |

## Transitions by relationship

| Relationship | Useful words or phrases | Verify before selecting |
|---|---|---|
| Addition | also, moreover, in addition, furthermore | The second point adds rather than contradicts. |
| Similarity | similarly, likewise, in the same way | The two cases share the relevant trait. |
| Contrast | however, by contrast, conversely, on the other hand | Identify exactly what reverses or differs. |
| Concession | nevertheless, nonetheless, still, even so | A result occurs despite a reason to expect otherwise. |
| Replacement | instead, rather | The new action or explanation substitutes for the first. |
| Result or conclusion | therefore, consequently, accordingly, as a result | The first statement explains or supports the second. |
| Example or specification | for example, for instance, specifically, in particular | The second statement instantiates or narrows the first. |
| Restatement | in other words, that is | The second makes the same point more clearly. |
| Time | previously, meanwhile, later, subsequently | Check order, simultaneity and tense clues. |
| Emphasis | indeed, notably, in fact | The added point reinforces or sharpens what precedes it; context may still signal contrast. |

**Worked contrast:** “The guide accurately predicts where the bus stops. **However,** it does not tell riders when it stops.” The second sentence points out a limitation, not an example of location accuracy.
