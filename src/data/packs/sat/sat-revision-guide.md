# Ratio SAT Revision Guide

Original Ratio revision notes for the **digital SAT** (taken in the Bluebook app). Official College Board pages are linked rather than copied; always check them for the latest rules.

---

## 1. The test at a glance

| Section | Modules | Questions | Time | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading and Writing | 2 × 27 | 54 | 2 × 32 min = 64 min | ~71 s |
| *Break* | | | 10 min | |
| Math | 2 × 22 | 44 | 2 × 35 min = 70 min | ~95 s |
| **Total** | 4 | **98** | **2 h 14 min** + break | |

- **Adaptive by section.** Module 1 has a mix of easy, medium and hard questions. How you do in Module 1 decides whether Module 2 is the easier or the harder version. Only the harder Module 2 gives access to the top of the score range, so **Module 1 accuracy matters most**.
- **Scores.** Each section is scored 200–800; the total is 400–1600. There is **no penalty for wrong answers**: never leave a question blank.
- **Within a module** you can move back and forth, flag questions to revisit, and use the answer eliminator. Once a module's time ends, you cannot go back to it.
- **Built-in tools:** a countdown timer, the Desmos graphing calculator (Math), the reference sheet (Math), highlighting and annotation (Reading and Writing).
- **Question types:** every Reading and Writing question is 4-option multiple choice with its own short passage (25–150 words). About 75% of Math questions are multiple choice; the rest are **student-produced responses (SPR)**, where you type the answer.

Official: [Test structure](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure) · [How scores are calculated](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/what-scores-mean/how-scores-calculated)

## 2. Reading and Writing

Questions in each module appear in domain order: Craft and Structure → Information and Ideas → Standard English Conventions → Expression of Ideas. Within each group they go roughly from easier to harder.

| Domain | Share | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| Craft and Structure | ~28% (13–15 Qs) | Words in Context, Text Structure and Purpose, Cross-Text Connections |
| Information and Ideas | ~26% (12–14 Qs) | Central Ideas and Details, Command of Evidence (textual and quantitative), Inferences |
| Standard English Conventions | ~26% (11–15 Qs) | Boundaries, Form, Structure, and Sense |
| Expression of Ideas | ~20% (8–12 Qs) | Transitions, Rhetorical Synthesis |

Official: [Reading and Writing overview](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/reading-writing)

### 2.1 Craft and Structure
**Words in Context**
1. Cover the choices. Read the text and predict your own word for the blank.
2. Find the clue: a contrast signal (*although, however, yet, unlike*) means the word flips the nearby idea; a colon or "in fact" means it restates it.
3. Pick the choice closest to your prediction. Check the tone (positive/negative) and strength.
- Trap: a word that fits the topic but not the logic, or a familiar meaning of a word with several (e.g. *sound* = reliable; *arrested* = stopped).

**Text Structure and Purpose**
- *Main purpose:* ask "Why did the author write this?" Use a verb: *to explain, to argue, to describe, to challenge*.
- *Function of underlined sentence:* say what the sentence does for the sentences around it (gives an example, raises a problem, introduces a counterpoint).
- *Overall structure:* summarize each sentence in 3–4 words, then match the sequence.
- Trap: choices that are true about the topic but too broad, too narrow, or not mentioned.

**Cross-Text Connections**
1. Summarize each text's claim in one line.
2. Decide the relationship: agrees, disagrees, or **qualifies** (agrees in part, adds a limit). "Qualifies" is the most common correct pattern.
3. Answer from the named author's point of view, using only what that text says.

### 2.2 Information and Ideas
**Central Ideas and Details:** the main idea covers the whole text, not one sentence. For detail questions, the answer is stated or closely paraphrased in the text.

**Command of Evidence (textual):** identify the exact claim, then pick the finding or quotation that would most *directly* show it. Ask: "If this were true, would it prove the claim?" Watch for evidence that is on-topic but proves a different point.

**Command of Evidence (quantitative):** read the title, axes or column headings and units first. The correct choice must (1) be accurate to the data and (2) support the specific claim in the text. Many wrong choices are accurate but irrelevant.

**Inferences:** the answer is the most logical next step from the information given, never a leap. Use the last sentence's setup ("This suggests that…") and eliminate anything that goes beyond the evidence.

### 2.3 Standard English Conventions
**Boundaries (punctuation)**

| Joining… | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Two independent clauses (IC + IC) | `IC; IC` · `IC, and IC` · `IC. IC` · `IC: IC` (if 2nd explains 1st) | `IC, IC` (comma splice) · `IC IC` (run-on) |
| Introductory phrase/clause + IC | `When it rained, the…` | semicolon or colon after a dependent clause |
| Nonessential information | a matching pair: `, … ,` or `— … —` or `( … )` | mixing a comma with a dash |
| Essential information (e.g. the title that identifies which book) | no commas | commas around it |
| Introducing a list or explanation | `IC: list` | colon after an incomplete clause (`such as: …`) |
| Subject and its verb | nothing between them | a single comma between subject and verb |
| Items in a list that already contain commas | semicolons between items | commas only |

**Form, Structure, and Sense (grammar)**
- **Subject–verb agreement:** find the real subject; skip prepositional phrases (*the collection of letters **is***). *Each, every, neither, either* are singular. With *neither…nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
- **Pronouns:** match number (*the team… its*), and avoid unclear pronouns. *it's* = it is; *its* = possessive; *their/there/they're*.
- **Verb tense:** match the timeline. Past perfect (*had + participle*) for the earlier of two past events.
- **Modifiers:** an introductory phrase describes the noun right after the comma (*Walking through Petra, **the visitors**…*).
- **Logical comparisons:** compare like with like (people with people, paintings with paintings).
- **Plurals and possessives:** *the bridge's cables*, *the scientists' findings*.

### 2.4 Expression of Ideas
**Transitions:** cover the choices and decide the relationship between the sentences first.

| Relationship | Common transitions |
|---|---|
| Contrast | however, by contrast, nevertheless, even so, still, yet |
| Addition | moreover, in addition, furthermore, also, similarly, likewise |
| Cause/effect | therefore, thus, as a result, consequently, accordingly, hence |
| Example | for example, for instance, specifically |
| Clarification | in other words, that is, in fact, indeed |
| Sequence/time | then, later, finally, meanwhile, at the same time, previously |

**Rhetorical Synthesis:** read the **goal** first, then pick the choice that achieves it. Ignore the notes until you need them. The correct choice may leave out true information; it just has to do what the goal asks (e.g. "emphasize a similarity", "introduce to an unfamiliar audience", "present the study and its finding").

## 3. Math

| Domain | Share | Main topics |
|---|---|---|
| Algebra | ~35% (13–15 Qs) | Linear equations in 1 and 2 variables, linear functions, systems of 2 linear equations, linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | ~35% (13–15 Qs) | Equivalent expressions, nonlinear equations and systems, nonlinear (quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, radical, absolute value) functions |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ~15% (5–7 Qs) | Ratios, rates, units, percentages, one- and two-variable data, probability, sample statistics and margin of error, studies and experiments |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ~15% (5–7 Qs) | Area and volume, lines and angles, triangles and similarity, right-triangle trigonometry, circles |

A calculator is allowed on **every** Math question. Official: [Math overview](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/math/overview) · [Calculator policy](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/calculator-policy)

### 3.1 Student-produced responses (SPR)
- Up to **5 characters** for a positive answer, **6** with a negative sign (the minus sign does not count toward the 5).
- Fractions and decimals are both fine: `3/4` or `.75`. Do not enter mixed numbers (`3 1/2` → enter `7/2` or `3.5`).
- If a decimal is too long, fill all the space: `2/3` → `.6666` or `.6667` (not `.67`).
- Do not enter symbols like `%`, `$` or commas.
- If more than one answer is correct, enter just one.

### 3.2 Algebra essentials
- Slope: m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Slope-intercept: y = mx + b. Point-slope: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Standard: Ax + By = C has slope −A/B.
- Parallel lines: equal slopes. Perpendicular: slopes are negative reciprocals (m₁ · m₂ = −1).
- In context, **slope = rate per unit**; **y-intercept = starting/fixed value**.
- Systems: **one solution** (different slopes), **no solution** (same slope, different intercept), **infinitely many** (same line, every coefficient proportional).
- Inequalities: flip the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative. "At most" ≤, "at least" ≥, "more than" >.
- An equation ax + b = cx + d has infinitely many solutions when a = c and b = d; no solution when a = c and b ≠ d.

### 3.3 Advanced Math essentials
- Factoring patterns: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b); (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b².
- Quadratic formula: x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a. **Discriminant** b² − 4ac: > 0 two real solutions, = 0 one, < 0 none.
- Sum of roots = −b/a; product of roots = c/a.
- Vertex form y = a(x − h)² + k: vertex (h, k); a > 0 opens up (minimum k), a < 0 opens down (maximum k). Vertex x = −b/2a. The vertex lies midway between the x-intercepts.
- Factored form y = a(x − p)(x − q): x-intercepts p and q.
- Exponential: y = a(1 + r)^t (growth), y = a(1 − r)^t (decay). "Doubles every k years" → a · 2^(t/k). The base shows the percent change: 0.85 means −15% per period.
- Exponent rules: xᵃ · xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ; (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ; x^(m/n) = ⁿ√(xᵐ); x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ.
- Radical and rational equations: always check for **extraneous solutions**.
- Function transformations: f(x) + k up k; f(x − h) right h; −f(x) reflect over x-axis.

### 3.4 Problem-Solving and Data Analysis essentials
- Percent change = (new − old)/old × 100%. Successive changes multiply: +20% then −20% = 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96.
- Reverse percent: after a 30% increase the value is 1.3 × original.
- Unit conversion: multiply by fractions equal to 1 (e.g. 1 km/1,000 m) so units cancel.
- Mean = sum ÷ count. Median = middle value (average of the two middle values for an even count). Outliers pull the mean more than the median.
- Standard deviation measures spread; data bunched near the mean has a smaller SD.
- Conditional probability from a table: **restrict to the given row or column** and use its total as the denominator.
- Margin of error: plausible range for the **population** value; larger random samples give smaller margins of error.
- **Random sampling** → results generalize to the population sampled. **Random assignment** → cause-and-effect conclusions. Neither → association only.

### 3.5 Geometry and Trigonometry essentials
The Bluebook reference sheet gives area/volume formulas and special right triangles. Know these without looking:
- Triangle angles sum to 180°; vertical angles are equal; a straight line is 180°; parallel lines cut by a transversal give angles that are equal or supplementary.
- Similar triangles: corresponding sides are proportional.
- Pythagorean theorem a² + b² = c². Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25.
- 30°-60°-90°: sides x, x√3, 2x. 45°-45°-90°: sides x, x, x√2.
- SOH-CAH-TOA. For complementary angles, sin A = cos(90° − A).
- Radians: π radians = 180°. Arc length = (θ/360°) · 2πr = rθ (θ in radians). Sector area = (θ/360°) · πr² = ½r²θ.
- Circle: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². If the equation is expanded, **complete the square** for x and for y.
- Volume of a cylinder πr²h; cone ⅓πr²h; sphere ⁴⁄₃πr³; prism lwh.

## 4. Strategy

### 4.1 Pacing
- **R&W:** about 70 seconds per question. Do every question in order; if one takes more than ~90 seconds, guess, flag and return.
- **Math:** about 95 seconds per question. Do quick ones first; flag long ones. Leave 2–3 minutes at the end to revisit flags.
- Never leave a blank: guess before time runs out.

### 4.2 Module 1 is the key
Because Module 1 decides your route, accuracy on Module 1 easy and medium questions is worth more than speed. Double-check those before moving on.

### 4.3 Use Desmos well
- Graph both sides of an equation and find the intersection.
- Solve systems by graphing both lines.
- Enter tables to find lines or curves of best fit.
- Use sliders to test which constant makes something true.
- Still write down your setup: Desmos is a tool, not a substitute for reading the question.

### 4.4 Eliminate actively
In Reading and Writing, three wrong choices usually fail for clear reasons: off-topic, too extreme, contradicted by the text, or true but not responsive. Strike them with the answer eliminator.

## 5. Review method: the error log
After every Ratio mock or drill, log each wrong or guessed question:

| Question ID | Domain / skill | Why I missed it | What I'll do next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| m1rw1-13 | Command of Evidence: Quantitative | Picked a true data point that didn't support the claim | Re-read the claim before checking the data |

Common "why" codes: **C** content gap, **R** misread the question, **T** ran out of time, **S** careless slip, **G** guessed. Review the log weekly; redo the matching Ratio drill for any skill with three or more entries.

## 6. Suggested 8-week study plan

| Week | Focus | Ratio | Official |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose | Ratio Mock 1 under timed conditions; start error log | Install [Bluebook](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/students/download-bluebook) and try the test preview |
| 2 | R&W conventions + Algebra | SEC drill, Algebra drill | [Khan Academy](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/khan-academy) units for weak skills |
| 3 | R&W evidence + Advanced Math | Information and Ideas drill, Advanced Math drill | [Student Question Bank](https://mypractice.collegeboard.org/questionbank/search) filtered by skill |
| 4 | First official test | Review error log | [Bluebook practice test](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests/bluebook) 1, then [My Practice](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/my-practice-101) review |
| 5 | Craft and Structure + PSDA | Craft and Structure drill, PSDA drill | Question Bank: hard questions in weak skills |
| 6 | Expression of Ideas + Geometry | Expression of Ideas drill, Geometry drill | Bluebook practice test 2 |
| 7 | Full mock + targeted repair | Ratio Mock 2 timed; redo missed drill questions | Bluebook practice test 3 |
| 8 | Final review | Re-read this guide's tables; light drills only | One more Bluebook test early in the week; check the [test-day checklist](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do) |

Short on time? Do weeks 1, 4, 7 and 8, plus drills for your two weakest domains.
