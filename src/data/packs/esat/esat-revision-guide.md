# ESAT Revision Guide

Engineering and Science Admissions Test (UAT-UK). This guide is original revision material written to the official ESAT Content Specification. It is not official UAT-UK material; always check the official pages linked at the end.

## 1. The test at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Modules | Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2, Physics, Chemistry, Biology |
| Per module | 27 multiple-choice questions, 40 minutes, separately timed |
| Time per question | about 89 seconds on average |
| Calculator / dictionary | Not allowed |
| Marking | 1 mark per correct answer, no negative marking |
| Results | Reported separately for each module (scale 1.0 to 9.0) |
| Delivery | On screen at Pearson VUE test centres |
| Which modules | Most courses need Mathematics 1 plus two further modules. Requirements differ by course: check the official course list. |

Mathematics 1 content (M) is assumed knowledge for every module. Mathematics 2 adds the MM topics.

## 2. Choosing modules

- Use the official course list (link below) for each course you apply to. Take every module any of your choices needs.
- Typical patterns: Engineering usually needs Mathematics 1, Physics and Mathematics 2. Natural Sciences routes differ by subject (physical vs biological). Do not guess: check the list.
- Each module is scored separately, so a weak module does not drag down a strong one, but every required module is seen.

## 3. Timing strategy (40 minutes, 27 questions)

1. **First pass (about 25 minutes):** answer every question you can do in under 60 seconds. Flag the rest.
2. **Second pass (about 12 minutes):** return to flagged questions, the most promising first.
3. **Final 3 minutes:** make sure every question has an answer. There is no negative marking, so a blank is always a lost mark.
4. Checkpoints: question 9 by about 13 minutes, question 18 by about 27 minutes.
5. If a question is still unclear after 90 seconds, eliminate what you can, pick the best answer, flag it, and move on.

## 4. Working without a calculator

- Keep numbers in standard form and cancel before multiplying: 4.2 × 10³ × 50 ÷ 1400 = 4.2 × 50 ÷ 1.4 = 3 × 50 = 150.
- Know the squares to 20², the cubes to 10³, powers of 2 to 2¹⁰ = 1024, and √2 ≈ 1.41, √3 ≈ 1.73, √5 ≈ 2.24, π ≈ 3.14.
- Estimate first, then use the answer options: they are often far enough apart that a rough estimate picks one out.
- Convert units at the start (minutes to seconds, cm³ to dm³, kJ to J, g to kg).
- Check a factor of 2 last: halving and doubling (round trip echoes, ½mv², ½ × base × height, the 2 in 2gh) is the most common slip.
- Write fractions rather than decimals when the numbers are awkward (1/3, 2/3).

## 5. Common traps by module

### Mathematics 1
- Successive percentage changes multiply, never add (+25% then −20% is ×1.25 × 0.8 = ×1).
- Area scale factor is k², volume scale factor is k³. From an area ratio, take the square root first.
- Bounds: the upper bound of a product uses both upper bounds; of a quotient, the upper bound over the lower bound.
- Truncation intervals differ from rounding intervals (3.7 truncated means 3.7 ≤ x < 3.8).
- Probability "without replacement" changes the second denominator; "given that" restricts the sample space.
- Inequalities: reverse the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative.
- Correlation does not imply causation.

### Mathematics 2
- Never divide both sides of an equation by an expression that can be zero (sin x, x): factorise instead, or you lose solutions.
- Logarithm equations: check every solution against the domain (log of a negative is undefined).
- Count trig solutions using the given interval, including or excluding the endpoints as stated.
- An integral below the x-axis is negative; an area is not. Split at roots when finding an area.
- Transformations: f(x − a) moves right by a; f(−x) reflects in the y-axis; af(x) stretches vertically.
- A repeated root means the line touches the curve: count distinct points carefully.
- Sum to infinity only exists when |r| < 1.

### Physics
- Velocity is a vector: a rebound is a change of (u + v), not (u − v).
- Elastic energy and kinetic energy are quadratic (∝ x², ∝ v²); doubling the variable quadruples the energy.
- Echoes travel there and back: halve the total distance.
- Power line losses: use I²R with the line current, not V²/R with the transmission voltage.
- Frequency never changes on refraction; speed and wavelength do.
- Pressure at depth: add atmospheric pressure if the question asks for total pressure.
- Percentage efficiency: input = useful ÷ efficiency.

### Chemistry
- Always find the limiting reagent before using a mole ratio.
- Use the balancing numbers (1 : 2 for H₂SO₄ : NaOH; triprotic acids release three H⁺).
- Bond energies: ΔH = bonds broken − bonds made; count every bond (2 H–Cl, not 1).
- Calorimetry: the mass in q = mcΔT is the mass of solution, not of the solid; a temperature rise means ΔH is negative.
- Electrolysis of aqueous solutions: H₂ forms at the cathode unless the metal is less reactive than hydrogen; O₂ forms at the anode unless a halide is concentrated.
- Catalysts change the rate, not the position of equilibrium or ΔH.
- Diluting an acid tenfold raises pH by 1; it never passes 7.

### Biology
- Osmosis is the movement of water from higher to lower water potential, across a partially permeable membrane.
- Meiosis gives four haploid, genetically different cells; mitosis gives two identical diploid cells.
- Genetic probabilities: an unaffected child of two carriers is a carrier with probability 2/3, not 1/2.
- DNA base pairing: A = T and G = C, so the four percentages are fixed by any one of them.
- Codons: three bases per amino acid, and the stop codon codes for none.
- Natural selection acts on variation that already exists; organisms do not mutate "in order to" adapt.
- Insulin lowers blood glucose (glucose → glycogen); glucagon raises it.
- Mark–release–recapture: N = (first sample × second sample) ÷ marked recaptures.

## 6. Topic checklists

Tick each topic when you can do a past-paper-style question on it in under 90 seconds without notes. Codes match the official ESAT Content Specification.

### Mathematics 1 (M1–M7)
- [ ] M1 Units: SI units, prefixes, compound units, conversions (area and volume)
- [ ] M2 Number: fractions, decimals, percentages, standard form, surds, indices, primes, HCF/LCM, bounds and error intervals, estimation, recurring decimals
- [ ] M3 Ratio and proportion: ratio sharing, direct and inverse proportion, percentage change, compound growth and decay, rates
- [ ] M4 Algebra: expanding, factorising, rearranging, algebraic fractions, linear and quadratic equations, completing the square, simultaneous equations, inequalities, sequences (linear and quadratic nth term), functions and graphs, gradients and equations of lines, circle x² + y² = r²
- [ ] M5 Geometry: angles, polygons, circle theorems, congruence and similarity, Pythagoras, trigonometry (including sine and cosine rules and exact values), area and volume (sectors, cones, spheres), transformations, vectors
- [ ] M6 Statistics: averages and spread, frequency tables, histograms, cumulative frequency, box plots, scatter graphs and correlation
- [ ] M7 Probability: tree diagrams, conditional probability, Venn diagrams, independent and mutually exclusive events, counting

### Mathematics 2 (MM1–MM8)
- [ ] MM1 Algebra and functions: indices, surds, quadratics and discriminant, simultaneous equations (linear and quadratic), inequalities, factor and remainder theorems
- [ ] MM2 Sequences and series: recurrence relations, Σ notation, arithmetic and geometric series, sum to infinity, binomial expansion
- [ ] MM3 Coordinate geometry: lines, parallel and perpendicular, circles (centre, radius, tangents), intersections
- [ ] MM4 Trigonometry: radians, arc length and sector area, exact values, graphs, sin²x + cos²x = 1, tan x = sin x / cos x, solving equations in a given interval
- [ ] MM5 Exponentials and logarithms: laws of logs, solving aˣ = b, exponential growth and decay
- [ ] MM6 Differentiation: polynomials and powers, tangents and normals, stationary points and their nature, increasing and decreasing functions, optimisation
- [ ] MM7 Integration: indefinite and definite integrals, finding the constant, area under a curve and between curves
- [ ] MM8 Graphs: sketching polynomials, transformations, using graphs to count solutions

### Physics (P1–P7)
- [ ] P1 Electricity: charge, current, potential difference, resistance, series and parallel circuits, I–V graphs (resistor, lamp, diode, thermistor, LDR), potential dividers, power and energy, mains electricity and cost
- [ ] P2 Magnetism: fields, electromagnets, motor effect (F = BIL), dc motors, electromagnetic induction, generators, transformers, the National Grid
- [ ] P3 Mechanics: motion graphs, SUVAT, forces and Newton's laws, weight, terminal velocity, Hooke's law and elastic energy, momentum and impulse, work, energy, power, efficiency
- [ ] P4 Thermal physics: conduction, convection, radiation, specific heat capacity, insulation
- [ ] P5 Matter: particle model, density, changes of state and latent heat, gas pressure and pV = constant, pressure in fluids
- [ ] P6 Waves: properties, v = fλ, reflection, refraction, sound and ultrasound, Doppler effect, electromagnetic spectrum and uses
- [ ] P7 Radioactivity: atomic structure, isotopes, alpha, beta and gamma decay and equations, properties and deflection, half-life, uses and hazards, fission and fusion

### Chemistry (C1–C17)
- [ ] C1 Atomic structure, isotopes, relative atomic mass, electron configuration
- [ ] C2 Periodic Table: groups, periods, trends
- [ ] C3 Chemical reactions, formulae and equations; reversible reactions and equilibrium
- [ ] C4 Quantitative chemistry: moles, empirical formulae, limiting reagents, gas volumes, concentration, titration, percentage yield, atom economy
- [ ] C5 Oxidation, reduction and redox; oxidation states
- [ ] C6 Bonding, structure and properties (ionic, covalent, metallic, giant and simple)
- [ ] C7 Group chemistry: Group 1, Group 17 (halogens and displacement), Group 18; transition metals
- [ ] C8 Separation techniques and chromatography (Rf values)
- [ ] C9 Acids, bases and salts; pH; neutralisation
- [ ] C10 Rates of reaction: collision theory, factors, activation energy, catalysts
- [ ] C11 Energetics: exothermic and endothermic, energy profiles, bond energies, calorimetry
- [ ] C12 Electrolysis: molten and aqueous, half-equations, electroplating
- [ ] C13 Carbon / organic chemistry: alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids, esters, isomers, addition and condensation polymers, cracking
- [ ] C14 Metals: reactivity series, extraction, displacement
- [ ] C15 Kinetic / particle theory
- [ ] C16 Chemical tests: gases, cations (flame tests, hydroxide precipitates), anions
- [ ] C17 Air and water: composition, pollutants, water treatment

### Biology (B1–B11)
- [ ] B1 Cells: prokaryotic and eukaryotic structure, specialisation, levels of organisation, microscopy
- [ ] B2 Movement across membranes: diffusion, osmosis, active transport
- [ ] B3 Cell division and sex determination: mitosis, cell cycle, meiosis, cancer, asexual vs sexual reproduction
- [ ] B4 Inheritance: alleles, genotype and phenotype, monohybrid crosses, pedigrees, probabilities
- [ ] B5 DNA: structure, genome, protein synthesis, mutations
- [ ] B6 Gene technologies: genetic engineering, stem cells, selective breeding
- [ ] B7 Variation: genetic and environmental, natural selection, evolution, antibiotic resistance
- [ ] B8 Enzymes: active site, temperature and pH, digestion enzymes
- [ ] B9 Animal physiology: respiration, nervous system and reflexes, circulation and the heart, gas exchange, digestion, kidneys, homeostasis (glucose, water), hormones and reproduction, disease and immunity
- [ ] B10 Ecosystems: relationships, nutrient cycles, sampling and population estimates, human impact
- [ ] B11 Plant physiology: photosynthesis and limiting factors, transport (xylem, phloem), transpiration

## 7. How to use the practice material

1. Revise one module topic by topic using the checklist and formula sheets.
2. Do the topic drill flashcards for that module until you can answer each card without hesitating.
3. Sit Mock A for the module under timed conditions (40 minutes, no calculator). Mark it and log every error in the error log.
4. Review the error log, revise the weak topics, then sit Mock B.
5. Move on to the official past papers (ENGAA and NSAA Section 1), skipping questions UAT-UK marks as outside the ESAT specification, and the official Pearson VUE practice tests to get used to the on-screen format.
6. In the final two weeks, sit full timed sets of your required modules back to back.
